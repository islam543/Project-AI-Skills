# Ground legal answers in authorities

Establish the applicable jurisdiction, the question and the relevant date. Identify which kinds of sources can support the answer, such as a current statute, historical regulation, official guidance or case. Distinguish the authority's text from your interpretation of its effect.

1. Bind each material claim to one or more retrieved evidence IDs. Resolve each ID to a source, version, part or section, and quotation or close paraphrase. Check what the passage actually supports and whether the source was in force at the relevant time.
2. State the rule, relevant exceptions and qualifications, then apply it to the facts supplied. Identify a missing material fact and explain how it could affect the conclusion.
3. When authorities conflict or the status of a source is unclear, give the competing text, dates and why it is unclear. Avoid asserting a hierarchy or definitive rule unless the evidence establishes it.
4. If retrieval lacks the relevant authority or the evidence cannot resolve the question, explain the gap. Ask for jurisdiction, date, facts or source material only when it could resolve the gap.

When testing a response format, include supported, unsupported, conflicting and outdated examples. Record separately whether the answer is correct, whether each claim is supported and whether each citation resolves.
